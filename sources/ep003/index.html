FigureValueAs ofSource
Desk: futures implied little chance of a move in Oct but "high probabilities" of a 25bp hike by year-end and two more by summer 2027. The Desk survey median expected one hike in Dec and one in Mar 2027, then a cut in early 2028. Term premiums may explain the gap between the two paths. The survey's longer-run fed funds estimate rose to about 3.4% ¶11—28 Oct 2026minutes ¶11
Desk: the higher-rate environment had already been weighing on equity prices and widening credit spreads before the period. Higher rates helped lower equity valuations. AI revenue misses and a hyperscaler's financing-heavy capex plan hit AI stocks; high-yield spreads on data-centre debt widened notably ¶12—28 Oct 2026minutes ¶12
Desk: Treasury yields rose significantly until early Oct on strong data, then partly reversed on AI-revenue doubts and French debt worries. Market functioning stayed orderly ¶10. Over the whole intermeeting period nominal yields were little changed ¶9—28 Oct 2026minutes ¶9–10
A majority of participants noted that financial conditions had tightened since the Sept meeting: higher mortgage rates, wider spreads for riskier and AI-related borrowers, less issuance. Many said this was weighing on housing, CRE, small business and other rate-sensitive sectors. Several still judged overall conditions supportive ¶37—28 Oct 2026minutes ¶37
Most backed the hold. Some said market-implied tightening for Dec and 2027 matched their own preferred path, which made holding less risky. A few called it a close call ¶41. Some wanted +25bp now ¶42, and a few of those could have accepted a hold if markets understood that more tightening was likely ¶42—28 Oct 2026minutes ¶41–42
Almost all participants saw further hikes, broadly in line with the Sept SEP, as likely appropriate. A couple said more than 25bp could be considered at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavourable ¶43—28 Oct 2026minutes ¶43
